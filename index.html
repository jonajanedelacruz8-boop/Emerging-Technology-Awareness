Topic 01

Artificial Intelligence (AI)

Artificial Intelligence refers to computer systems engineered to simulate human intelligence. This includes learning from past experience, understanding natural spoken language, recognizing complex images, and making autonomous decisions.

Everyday Example: Streaming Recommendations & Virtual Assistants

When watching videos on Netflix or YouTube, AI algorithms continuously process your watch history, search queries, and skips to recommend personalized content. Similarly, voice assistants like Apple Siri or Google Assistant use Natural Language Processing (NLP) to parse your voice and answer instantly.

Benefits of AI

  • Task Automation: Completes repetitive tasks rapidly without fatigue.
  • Rapid Analysis: Processes massive datasets in seconds.
  • Personalized Education: Adapts study material to individual student learning paces.
  • Medical Diagnostics: Assists medical staff in identifying anomalies in scans early.

Limitations & Risks

  • Algorithmic Bias: Learns and reinforces historical biases present in training data.
  • Job Displacement: Automates routine cognitive and administrative tasks.
  • Lack of Genuine Intuition: Lacks true emotional comprehension or moral reasoning.
  • Data Over-reliance: Requires extensive user personal data harvesting to train effectively.
Topic 02

Internet of Things (IoT)

The Internet of Things (IoT) is a global network of physical hardware devices—embedded with sensors, microcontrollers, and communication modules—that automatically gather, send, and act on data without human intervention.

Everyday Example: Smart Fitness Watches & Connected Appliances

A smartwatch (Apple Watch, Fitbit, or Xiaomi Band) continuously tracks heart rates, step counts, and sleep stages, automatically uploading health metrics to your cloud account. Another example is a smartphone-controlled smart thermostat (Nest) that adjusts cooling automatically when you leave home.

Benefits of IoT

  • Convenience: Instant remote control of home lighting, security, and climate.
  • Energy Efficiency: Automated power scheduling minimizes electrical waste.
  • Health Monitoring: Immediate notifications to relatives or medical services during emergencies.
  • Smart City Infrastructure: Dynamic traffic management and automated waste collection.

Limitations & Risks

  • Cybersecurity Threats: Weak default passwords on smart cameras leave networks exposed.
  • Network Dependency: Disconnected internet causes smart hardware to lose features.
  • Privacy Intrusion: Continuous background tracking enables corporate user profiling.
  • Interoperability Issues: Devices from rival manufacturers often fail to sync smoothly.
Topic 03

Cloud Computing & Big Data

Cloud Computing delivers on-demand computing services—including remote storage, processing power, and databases—over the internet. Big Data describes massive datasets that are computationally analyzed to uncover hidden trends, patterns, and behaviors.

Everyday Example: Google Drive & Waze / Google Maps Navigation

When uploading assignment documents to Google Drive, OneDrive, or iCloud, cloud computing allows you to open them on any device. Meanwhile, navigation tools like Waze or Google Maps collect real-time location and speed data from millions of users simultaneously (Big Data) to instantly calculate traffic delays and re-route drivers.

Benefits

  • Universal Access: Retrieve documents securely from any location with internet access.
  • Scalability: Expand digital storage instantly without buying physical hard drives.
  • Predictive Insights: Enables weather forecasting, epidemic tracking, and economic planning.
  • Real-time Collaboration: Multiple users editing shared documents online simultaneously.

Limitations & Risks

  • Service Outages: Data center downtime cuts off access to critical work files.
  • Data Breaches: Centralized cloud servers are high-priority targets for cyberattacks.
  • Recurring Costs: Subscription costs scale up as stored data volume expands.
  • Data Sovereignty: Ambiguity regarding which jurisdiction controls remote data server farms.
Topic 04

Responsible Use & Computing Ethics

As emerging technologies become woven into our daily existence, ethical awareness ensures we protect personal privacy, avoid digital misinformation, maintain academic integrity, and promote a safer cyber environment for everyone.

Digital Footprint & Personal Privacy

Every post, search query, location check-in, and app permission builds a permanent digital footprint. Responsible users regularly review app permissions, use strong authentication, and remain conscious of public data exposure.

Deepfakes & Misinformation

Generative AI tools can create ultra-realistic fake images, audio impersonations, and videos (deepfakes). Computing ethics requires critical media literacy—verifying sources before sharing content online to prevent harm.

Academic Integrity in the AI Era

Generative AI models should be used as study aids, research brainstormers, or tutors—never as a tool to bypass actual learning or pass off machine output as original personal student work.

Environmental Impact of Computing

Training complex AI models and powering massive cloud data servers demands substantial electricity and cooling resources. Sustainable technology adoption emphasizes energy-efficient computing and electronic waste recycling.
